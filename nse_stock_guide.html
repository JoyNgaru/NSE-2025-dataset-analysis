Step-by-Step Guide
How to Buy NSE Shares

You cannot buy shares directly — you need to set up two accounts first. Here's the full process from scratch to owning your first Kenyan stock.

  • 1
    Open a CDS Account (Central Depository System)
    This is your electronic "share ownership account" — like an M-Pesa account but for shares. Every share you buy is stored here as a digital record. Without this, you cannot legally own NSE shares.
    What you need:
    • Valid National ID or Passport
    • KRA PIN certificate (get from iTax if you don't have one)
    • Proof of residence — utility bill or bank statement
    • Passport-size photos (2–4)
    • Minimum deposit: typically KES 100–1,000

    Where to go: Any licensed stockbroker in Kenya opens this for you — Genghis Capital, Sterling Capital, AIB Capital, Standard Investment Bank, Dyer & Blair.
  • 2
    Choose a Stockbroker & Open a Trading Account
    A stockbroker is a licensed firm that buys and sells shares on your behalf on the NSE. You cannot trade directly — all transactions must go through them. Think of them as your "agent" on the stock exchange floor.
    Types of brokers:
    • Full-service brokers — Give advice, research, and execute trades. Higher fees but good for beginners (e.g. Genghis Capital, Dyer & Blair)
    • Online/discount brokers — Self-service platforms, lower fees. Good once you know what you're doing (e.g. Hisa app, AIB Capital)

    Fees to expect per transaction:
    • Brokerage commission: 1.3–2% of trade value
    • CDS fee: 0.12% (capped at KES 1,000)
    • NSE trading fee: 0.12%
    • Stamp duty: KES 20 flat
  • 3
    Fund Your Trading Account
    Before you can buy anything, your broker needs money in your account. Transfer funds from your bank or M-Pesa (where supported). Minimum to start meaningfully: KES 5,000–10,000.
    Payment methods accepted by most brokers:
    • Bank transfer via RTGS or EFT
    • M-Pesa (many modern brokers support this)
    • Cheque (takes longer to clear)
  • 4
    Place Your Buy Order
    Tell your broker what you want to buy, how many shares, and at what price. You can do this by phone, email, or through their app/online portal.
    Example: "Buy 500 shares of Co-operative Bank (COOP) at market price."

    Two types of orders:
    • Market order — Buy immediately at the current price. Simpler, executes fast.
    • Limit order — Only buy if the price drops to your target (e.g. "Buy COOP only if it hits KES 10.00"). May not execute if price never reaches your target.
  • 5
    Wait for Settlement (T+3)
    After you buy, the NSE has a "T+3" rule — your shares officially appear in your CDS account 3 business days after the transaction date. Your money leaves your account at the same time.
    • T+3 = Trade date + 3 business days
    • You'll receive a contract note from your broker confirming the trade
    • Shares will appear in your CDS statement after settlement
  • 6
    Monitor & Collect Dividends
    Once you own shares, you're an investor. Check prices periodically, review company announcements, and collect dividends — your share of company profits paid out to shareholders.
    Dividend cycle for the top 3 picks:
    • Announced: March–May (with annual results)
    • Paid: June–August
    • You must own shares before the "book closure date" to qualify

    Tax to know:
    • Dividends: 5% withholding tax (deducted automatically)
    • Capital gains (profit when you sell): 0.3% tax

    Where to check prices: NSE website (nse.co.ke), your broker's app, or financial sites like Investing.com

Practical Beginner Tips
Things to Know Before You Start
💵 Start Small

You can start with as little as KES 5,000–10,000. Buy 100–200 shares of one company first to learn the process before scaling up.

📅 Use Dollar-Cost Averaging

Split your investment across 3–4 months instead of buying all at once. This protects you from bad timing and smooths out price fluctuations.

🎁 Collect Dividends

All top 3 beginner picks pay dividends. Announced March–May, paid June–August. Must own shares before "book closure date" to qualify.

🧘 Don't Panic Sell

Short-term dips are normal — even good stocks drop 10–15% sometimes. The 3-year trend shows consistent growth. Patience is your greatest asset.

🌍 Diversify

Never put all your money in one stock. Spread across at least 5–7 companies from different sectors so one bad performer doesn't wipe you out.

📊 Review Quarterly

Companies publish results every 3 months. Read headline summaries — profit up or down? These move share prices. Your broker will send updates.

⚠️ Important Warnings

  • KPLC & PORT: Despite 500%+ growth, these stocks can drop 20–30% in a single month. Only invest money you can afford to lose.
  • Penny stocks (HAFR, SMER, CABL): Low prices attract beginners, but low trading volumes mean you might struggle to sell when you want to.
  • Momentum can break: High-growth stocks in 2025 may slow in 2026. If the 2025 momentum breaks, losses can be severe and quick.

Plain English Definitions
Beginner's Glossary

Stock market terms can feel intimidating. Here's what the key words actually mean.

Share / Stock
A tiny piece of ownership in a company. If EABL has 1 billion shares and you own 1,000 of them, you own 0.0001% of the company.
NSE (Nairobi Securities Exchange)
Kenya's official stock exchange — the marketplace where shares of Kenyan companies are bought and sold.
CDS Account
Central Depository System — your digital account where NSE shares are stored electronically. Like a bank account, but for shares.
Stockbroker
A licensed firm that buys and sells shares on your behalf. You cannot trade on the NSE directly — all orders go through a broker.
Dividend
A portion of a company's profits paid to shareholders. If COOP earns KES 10 billion profit, it may distribute KES 1 per share to all shareholders.
Volatility
How much a share price swings up and down. High volatility = dramatic price swings. Low volatility = gradual, predictable movement.
Market Order
A buy instruction that says "buy now at whatever the current price is." Fast and guaranteed to execute.
Limit Order
A buy instruction that says "only buy if the price drops to X." May never execute if the price doesn't reach your target.
T+3 Settlement
After buying shares, they appear in your CDS account 3 business days later. This is NSE's official settlement window.
Book Closure Date
The deadline by which you must own shares to qualify for a dividend. If you buy after this date, you miss that round of dividends.
Capital Gain
The profit you make when you sell a share for more than you paid. E.g., bought at KES 10, sold at KES 15 = KES 5 capital gain per share.
Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)
Splitting your investment across multiple months to average out your purchase price, reducing the impact of buying at a bad time.
